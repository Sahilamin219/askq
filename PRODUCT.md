# Future product: the family's health memory

**Today** most families keep their health history in one person's head, usually a mother's, plus a WhatsApp gallery of
prescriptions. When someone falls sick at 11 pm, the questions are always the same: *is this serious, can it wait till
morning, what happened last time?*

**North star:** a health companion for the whole household that remembers everything, knows each person, and knows when to
hand over to a human.

- **Always-on triage for every member.** Kids, parents and grandparents, each with their own risk rules. Infants and elderly people with chronic illness escalate earlier.
- **Longitudinal memory.** Episodes become a timeline per person: what was tried, what worked, which doctor saw it. "Same as last winter?" becomes answerable.
- **Plug-ins on the existing hooks.** Read lab reports and prescriptions from photos, pull records via ABHA/ABDM, sync wearables (BP, glucose, SpO₂), add a clinician-curated knowledge base.
- **Seamless handoff.** The doctor receives a structured case summary instead of a chat log, replies inside the same thread, and the episode closes with their plan.
- **Proactive care.** Follow-up check-ins ("Is Aarav's fever down today?"), medicine and vaccination reminders, chronic-care nudges for Papa's sugar readings.
- **Caregiver mode.** Adult children far away can co-manage an elderly parent's health with shared visibility and alerts.

**Where it fits:** direct-to-consumer apps; insurers (cheaper first-line triage, fewer unnecessary ER visits); employers'
family health benefits; telemedicine clinics as their intake layer; ASHA and community health workers managing many
households, in voice and local languages.

**Moat:** the household's longitudinal record and the trust that the assistant will escalate when it matters.
